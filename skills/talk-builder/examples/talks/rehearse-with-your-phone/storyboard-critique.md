# Storyboard critique: Rehearse with your phone

Throughline as read: "Your phone is the rehearsal coach you already own." (9 words; it holds,
but no slide says it yet.)

## Curse of knowledge

- Slide 3 "My setup: tripod, OBS, Whisper, WPM": jargon. OBS, Whisper and WPM mean nothing to
  most of this room. Show a phone on a stack of books instead; say "words per minute" once.
- Slide 3: skipped steps. It jumps from "record" to tooling. The room needs "watch it back"
  before it needs any tool.
- Slide 4 "Metrics": abstraction. Name the three things to count, with one example each.
- Slide 2 "Why rehearsal matters": assumed buy-in. The room has not agreed it has a problem
  yet; start from the moment it hurts to watch yourself.

## Structure

A flat list (agenda, why, setup, metrics, steps, questions) with no tension. Rebuild it as
problem → solution → proof → next step: the wince of the first recording, the phone as the
fix, three run-throughs as proof, and one ask. Open with a question the room answers "yes"
to, not an agenda. Close on the next step and the guide, not "Questions?" (there is no Q&A).

## Audience fit

Junior developers who have never filmed themselves: no tools on the slides, one cheap setup,
and the guide carries the details.

## Top fixes

1. Cut the agenda; open with "Who has watched a video of themselves speak?"
2. Replace the setup slide with one picture of a phone at eye height.
3. Turn "Metrics" into "count three things" with the three named.
4. Add a black slide before the close for the room's answer.
5. End on the one next step and the guide's QR code.

The speaker confirmed the revised storyboard below.

1. Watching yourself speak is the fastest way to get better.
2. It hurts once, then it helps.
3. A phone at eye height is all the kit you need.
4. Count three things, not thirty.
5. Three recorded run-throughs are enough to hear the difference.
6. (black) Which habit would you fix first?
7. Your next step: record one run-through this week.
